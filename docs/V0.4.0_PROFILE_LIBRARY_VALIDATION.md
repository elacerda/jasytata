## Gate 6C geometry-blocker resolution — 2026-09-30

The evidence audit completed before the production data was changed. It found
primary-source conventions that support the already-selected **approximate
nominal envelope** semantics. This applies the unchanged Gate 3 rule: vertices
are generated only when a source defines the dimensions and boundary convention;
no regular hexagon is inferred from an ambiguous, unqualified diameter.

### MaNGA

The primary source is Drory et al. 2015, “The MaNGA Integral Field Unit Fiber
Feed System for the Sloan 2.5 m Telescope,” *AJ* 149:77,
[DOI 10.1088/0004-6256/149/2/77](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-6256/149/2/77),
[full article](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.1535). Section 3.2.1 defines the
hexagonal ring packing and gives the fiber-count relation; §3.2.2 describes the
hexagonal ferrule; §3.2.3 and Fig. 6 discuss measured packing/metrology
deviations from intended positions in an ideal fiber hexagon. Section 3.2.5
introduces the selected bundle complement. Table 4 is verified directly:

| Fibers | Rings | Corner (arcsec) | Flat (arcsec) | `Corner × √3/2` (arcsec) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 19 | 2 | 12 | 10.4 | 10.392 |
| 37 | 3 | 17 | 14.7 | 14.722 |
| 61 | 4 | 22 | 19.0 | 19.053 |
| 91 | 5 | 27 | 23.3 | 23.383 |
| 127 | 6 | 32 | 27.7 | 27.713 |

The table labels its dimensions `Corner` and `Flat`; the following text says they
are angular edge-to-edge distances of the active cores at 60 μm per arcsecond.
Interpreting `Corner` as the ideal active-core envelope's corner-to-corner span
and `Flat` as its flat-to-flat span matches that geometry. For a regular hexagon,
`flat = corner × √3/2`; the differences in the table are all below 0.083″.
That is consistent with the printed precision (whole-arcsecond `Corner`,
0.1-arcsecond `Flat`) and does not establish finer precision than the paper
reports. `Corner` is authoritative for vertex construction because the source
names the corner extent explicitly; `Flat` is an independent consistency check,
not a second fit or an averaged dimension.

The Jasytata footprint is therefore an **approximate nominal envelope polygon**,
not the exact observed-area union of apertures. This is the selected Gate 2
`nominal_envelope` fidelity. The paper's ideal-hexagon reference supports using
that nominal construction while the measured deviations, rounded angular sizes,
120:132:150 μm core/clad/buffer dimensions and per-fiber metrology remain
limitations. The canonical vertex-on-north orientation is only a local
coordinate convention; Gate 5 PA behavior remains `not_applicable` and no
physical sky PA is inferred. All five frozen MaNGA profiles and their existing
three-position strategies became bundleable. Their sequence identifiers,
offsets, ordering and `effective_sequence` default were not changed. The profiles
describe individual bundles only, not plate coverage or fibre assignment.

### CALIFA / PPAK

The audit inspected Kelz et al. 2006, “PMAS: The Potsdam Multi Aperture
Spectrophotometer. II. The Wide Integral Field Unit PPak,”
[DOI 10.1086/497455](https://doi.org/10.1086/497455),
[full article](https://arxiv.org/pdf/astro-ph/0512557); the [official CAHA
PMAS/PPAK specifications](https://www.caha.es/pmas/PMAS_OVERVIEW/pmas_overview.html)
and [geometrical-layout figure](https://www.caha.es/pmas/PMAS_OVERVIEW/pmas_overview_Fig3b-x.html);
and Martinsson et al. 2013, “The DiskMass Survey VI,”
[full article](https://arxiv.org/html/1307.8130). Kelz identifies 331 object
fibres, 2.7″-diameter cores, a hexagonal field, 74″ maximum field diameter and
about 60% fill; its table reports 74″ × 64″. CAHA's current overview gives
74″ × 65″ and links the geometry-and-size figure of the 331-science-fibre
central IFU. Martinsson §4.1 states that the 331 science cores form a regular
hexagonal grid with a 64″ × 74″ diagonal field and 60% fill.

Martinsson §4.1.1 further says the as-built grid is not perfectly regular and
describes a post-fabrication astrometric table measured from a back-lit direct
image. **Appendix C Table C.1 was recovered** in the article's full HTML/PDF: it
tabulates X/Y positions in arcseconds for the 331 science fibres and 36 sky
fibres relative to the central science fibre. No separate supplementary or
official machine-readable file was located during this audit. The table is
as-built center evidence; it is not substituted for the selected nominal
envelope, and it carries no Jasytata claim of a precision aperture union.

The chosen boundary is the **nominal ideal envelope**: use the explicitly
identified 74″ maximum diameter as corner-to-corner, so `R = 37″`; an ideal
regular hexagon then has flat-to-flat extent `74 × √3/2 = 64.085″`, consistent
with the source's rounded 64″ value. The CAHA page's 65″ and Martinsson's
measured deviations are retained as precision/fidelity limits. The profile is
not the as-built union of 331 circular science apertures and is not the
reconstructed/dithered CALIFA field. The existing three-point CALIFA sequence
is represented separately from one-exposure instrument geometry; its effective
geometric union does not assert reconstructed cube completeness. The profile
`califa-pmas-ppak-331` and strategy `califa-ppak-three-point` are now bundled.
No fiber pitch, invented coordinate list, camera PA, lattice, or fill-completeness
claim was added. Reported pitch values differ within Kelz et al., and pitch was
not used to construct the envelope: §2.2 states 3.6″, while Table 5 lists 3.5″.
Of the representations considered, the frozen contract selects option 2: a
nominal polygon envelope from the
source-defined boundary. It does not select an exact/near-exact compound union
of measured circles. Appendix C gives measured centers but no per-center
uncertainties; such an as-built aperture union would also be a different profile
semantics from the selected single-exposure nominal envelope.
