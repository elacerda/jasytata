# v0.4.0 empirical instrument matrix

## Scope, evidence and interpretation

Research date: **2026-09-29**. Development authority:
[v0.4.0 roadmap](V0.4.0_ROADMAP.md). Baseline:
`2b4e5d23bbc42d04b1155a4f7733575d58810b44`, published v0.3.0.
This is a requirements inventory and conservative capability assessment, not a
claim that all listed instruments are bundled or selected for release.

An instrument defines optics/focal-plane geometry and modes; a survey defines
targets, visits, placement, overlap, dithers and data policies. Facility instrument
cases below with no specified survey are explicitly instrument-only cases. No
synthetic survey is created to enable library selection.

All angular dimensions retain their source precision. `deg`, `arcmin` and
`arcsec` mean angular degrees, arcminutes and arcseconds; conversion is
1 deg = 60 arcmin = 3600 arcsec. Telescope aperture and fibre sizes in metres or
micrometres are physical dimensions, not footprint sizes. Areas quoted by sources
are not automatically geometric union areas; rounded field diameters, detector
area and optical envelopes must not be interchanged. Fill factors refer to the
stated instrument/definition, not a generic multiplier for coverage.

Current v2/runtime evidence was inspected in `types.ts`, `profiles/schema-v2.ts`,
`document.ts`, `registry.ts`, `v2.ts`, `footprints.ts`, and the profile tests,
after code-review-graph navigation. The existing contracts are documented in
[Schema v2](PROFILE_SCHEMA.md), [algorithms](ALGORITHM.md) and [PRODUCT](../PRODUCT.md):
local `[east,north]` degrees about an ICRS center; astronomical PA positive east
of north; rectangle/circle/simple polygon/nonrecursive compound; independent
two-vector lattice or manual survey; sampled binary area coverage. Registry
instrument registration is independent of surveys, but browser documents and
active output selection require a survey. A `TileRecord` PA/export value is not
automatically applied as a per-row geometry override by `footprintForTile`.

Classifications are engineering/scientific judgments derived from the cited
requirements, not classifications supplied by the sources:

- **A — v2 exact:** the stated planning field at a declared configuration/PA
  fits existing geometry and semantics without runtime/scientific changes.
  Exact representability does not remove baseline projection, sampling, rounding
  or observational calibration limits, and does not mean exact spherical area.
- **B — v0.4 core extension:** an identified minimum planning requirement needs
  a contract delta for the intended case. Polygonal hexagons and triangular
  placement alone do not qualify; v2 already represents them.
- **C — usable approximation:** a documented nominal/envelope field can support
  meaningful planning, with no claim of exact single-exposure active area, gaps,
  throughput, depth or completeness. No C profile is bundled in Gate 1.
- **D — deferred:** the stated full-pointing semantics require capabilities
  beyond the currently proposed v0.4 core. Narrower bundle/envelope workflows may
  be reconsidered explicitly; they do not establish support for the full case.

For **every case**, coordinate/export support currently means ICRS pointing
centers in Jasytata, not an observatory-ready observing block, fibre-assignment
file, FITS/WCS product or scheduling command. Unknown facility-specific formats,
PA-zero mappings and placement constants are recorded as unknown, never invented.
Exposure coverage is separate from spectral/depth completeness and guide-star
feasibility. Unless a case states otherwise, no intrinsic overlap or authoritative
automatic lattice was established; supplied/manual centers are the appropriate
initial workflow. Dither offsets are not a survey-wide lattice or overlap.

## Integral-field spectroscopy evidence

### CALIFA / PMAS-PPAK — B

PMAS is the instrument; PPAK its fibre-bundle mode; CALIFA the target survey.
The science envelope is hexagonal, 74″ × 64″, containing 331 fibres of 2.7″
diameter, with roughly 60% instantaneous fill. Additional sky/calibration fibres
are not part of contiguous target coverage. Three offset observations fill
inter-fibre holes for reconstruction. Thus an individual exposure and the final
CALIFA field are different planning objects. These values and the three-point
scheme are described in [Sánchez et al. 2016, DR3, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.02289);
the survey intent is in [Sánchez et al. 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.0962).

V2 can encode a hexagon as a polygon and individual fibres as compound circles.
It cannot describe the required observation sequence or distinguish reconstructed
field coverage from single-exposure sampling. This is the reason for B, not a
missing hexagonal lattice. Plan supplied galaxy centers; no survey overlap/lattice
is imposed. Bundle PA, precise offset convention, wavelength-dependent vignetting
and facility export mapping need confirmation before a final profile. The 74″ and
64″ numbers are approximate extents, not enough to infer exact regular vertices.

### SDSS-IV / MaNGA — B (individual bundle scope)

The MaNGA fibre feed supplies BOSS spectrographs on the Sloan telescope; the
survey assigns bundles to galaxies. The five science configurations contain
19/37/61/91/127 fibres, with nominal diameters 12″/17″/22″/27″/32″, across a
3°-diameter plate. The [SDSS fibre-feed description](https://www.sdss4.org/dr14/manga-instrument/)
lists the configurations and 54% live-core fill; [Law et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04285)
quotes 56% bundle fill. Retain this definition/source difference rather than
silently choosing a universal value. The [DR17 strategy](https://www.sdss4.org/dr17/manga/manga-survey-strategy/)
specifies 2″ cores, 0.5″ core-to-core gaps and a three-position equilateral
dither of side 1.44″.

Intended usable scope is one configured galaxy bundle with its dithered strategy,
not a uniformly covered plate. V2 polygons already describe nominal hexagons;
mode/size identity and visit/effective-coverage semantics motivate B. Full plate
allocation remains deferred. Use supplied target centers, actual bundle PA and
plate/IFU identifiers; no invented overlap or automatic galaxy grid. Precise
outer boundary, diameter convention and on-sky PA/metrology must be sourced for
each size. Existing Jasytata CSV is not an SDSS plate-design export.

### SDSS-V / LVM — B

LVM-I is the instrument/facility; LVM is the survey. The science telescope has a
nominal half-degree hexagonal IFU. Four 16 cm refractors serve science, two sky
fields and standards. The science IFU has 1801 circular 35.3″ apertures on 37″
pitch, giving 83% fill after its mask. K-mirrors derotate the science/sky fields.
See [Drory et al. 2024, §§IV.2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.01637v1).
These apertures must not be called a solid hexagonal exposure.

The [SDSS DR19 observing strategy](https://sdss.org/dr19/lvm/targeting/)
distinguishes Milky Way mosaics with one overlapping fibre row, nine-point dithers
for Magellanic Clouds and nearby galaxies, and sparse Orion outskirts at one-fifth
tile filling. No single universal overlap/coverage policy follows. A polygonal
envelope and triangular basis are already expressible; regional visit groups and
single-exposure versus dithered semantics justify B. Exact offset vectors,
half-degree diameter convention, derotator PA zero, authoritative sky tessellation
and tile/export identifiers remain to be frozen. Do not substitute an arbitrary
local triangular grid for the published all-sky tile list. Sky/standard pointings
are separate operational fields, not science-area additions.

### VLT / MUSE WFM — C

MUSE is a facility instrument, not a survey. WFM uses 24 IFUs to sample a
near-contiguous 1′ square, at 0.2″ spatial sampling, according to the
[ESO overview](https://www.eso.org/sci/facilities/paranal/instruments/muse/overview.html).
The [ESO p2 tutorial](https://www.eso.org/sci/observing/phase2/SMGuidelines/Documentation/p2TutorialMUSE.html)
gives 59.9″ × 60″ operational dimensions, recommends multiple exposures at PAs
separated by 90° and small offsets, and distinguishes on-target mosaicking from
off-target sky exposures. The tutorial's WFM pixel-scale entry differs from the
overview; it is not used to derive geometry here.

A nominal rectangle supports target/mosaic field planning; exact instantaneous
active-area/fill and on-sky boundaries were not established in this research.
Consequently C, not an exact detector-mask or gap-free-coverage claim. Spectral
trace gaps on a CCD must not be interpreted as sky gaps without a calibrated
mapping. PA and dither/rotation sequences belong to observations. Use manual
centers until a named survey's placement/overlap is sourced. Jasytata does not
export ESO OBs or assess guide-star/AO feasibility. A final nominal-field profile
need not await a detailed mask, provided the approximation is explicit.

### VLT / MUSE NFM — C

This is a distinct MUSE AO mode, not a smaller survey lattice. The
[ESO overview](https://www.eso.org/sci/facilities/paranal/instruments/muse/overview.html)
quotes 7.5″ × 7.5″ with 0.025″ sampling. The
[p2 tutorial](https://www.eso.org/sci/observing/phase2/SMGuidelines/Documentation/p2TutorialMUSE.html)
instead specifies 7.42″ × 7.43″, a guide star within 5″ of the center, and
rotation/dither observing examples. Source precision and actual usable boundary
must be reconciled; do not quietly bundle an exact 7.5″ square. No exact
single-exposure fill/mask was verified. C supports nominal small-field planning
with AO feasibility handled externally; the optical guide region is not science
coverage. Manual target centers, declared PA and programme-specific offsets are
appropriate. No intrinsic survey overlap, lattice or observatory export format
is inferred. Detailed masks can remain a refinement after semantics are agreed.

### CFHT / SITELLE — C

SITELLE is an imaging Fourier-transform spectrometer; SIGNALS is one scientific
programme using it, not an intrinsic geometry or tiling policy. CFHT describes a
contiguous nominal 11′ × 11′ field / 121 arcmin² and two imaging ports, not two
adjacent sky detectors: [instrument overview](https://www.cfht.hawaii.edu/Instruments/Sitelle/)
and [upgrade description](https://www.cfht.hawaii.edu/en/news/SITELLEUpgrade/).
Its [performance table](https://www.cfht.hawaii.edu/Instruments/Sitelle/SITELLE_specsinformation.php)
gives camera A 10.97′ × 11.06′ and B 10.95′ × 11.03′, 0.321″ mean scale,
field distortion reaching about 1.7% at 6′, north-up/east-left orientation and
fixed rotation within ±6 arcmin.

An 11′ rectangle is therefore a useful nominal-field approximation, not exact
port-union or common-port coverage. Usable area after registration/cropping and
the current calibrated boundary are unknown here. C requires documenting which
field the approximation means. An FTS scan supplies a cube rather than a sky
dither; no compulsory dither, overlap or lattice was established. Use supplied
centers and the fixed orientation, with residual orientation/calibration limits
explicit. No CFHT QSO submission/export or SIGNALS policy is bundled.

### Keck / KCWI — A (standard slicer fields at one declared PA)

KCWI on Keck II is an instrument, not a survey. Its current
[Keck primer](https://www2.keck.hawaii.edu/inst/kcwi/primer.html)
gives **measured** slicer dimensions: 20.4″ along the slices, with 24 slices
spanning 8.4″ (small), 16.5″ (medium), or 33.1″ (large) across them. These
standard contiguous IFU fields are rectangles, unlike fibre-bundle envelopes.
The [instrument design paper, §3.1](https://www2.keck.hawaii.edu/inst/kcwi/papers/martin_kcwi_spie2010.pdf)
explains the reflective slicer and why it avoids fibre-packing gaps. A K-mirror
compensates field rotation. Nod-and-shuffle, sky nods and nonstandard modes are
separate observing strategies and are outside these profiles.

The bundled local reference has **east width across slices**, **north height
20.4″ along slices**, and explicit **PA = 0° along slices**. This is a declared
planning orientation, not a claim about Keck `ROTPOSN`/`SKYPA` zero. The
[commissioning PA appendix](https://www2.keck.hawaii.edu/inst/kcwi/commissioning_plan.html)
proposes several reference axes with approximate offsets; its unfinished
calibration is insufficient for an automated keyword conversion. Use catalogues
whose centers and common orientation match the profile. Exact astrometric
distortion, slice-edge calibration and wavelength/depth completeness are not
claimed. No universal overlap/dither/lattice is established; no survey is bundled.
Mode-specific field areas are width × height, derived from those dimensions.

### Hector / Hector Galaxy Survey — D (full plate)

Hector is the AAT instrument; the galaxy survey selects targets/configurations.
The [official instrument page](https://hector.survey.org.au/instrument)
describes 21 bundles including calibration bundles, two spectrographs, robotic
plate positioning and nominal science sizes up to 27″. The newer
[Oh et al. 2025 paper, §§1, 2.2, 2.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25784v1)
describes a 2° field, bundle diameters of 12–26″ and seven 1800 s frames with
0.4–0.7″ offsets. Treat the differing diameter precision/configurations as an
uncertainty; do not turn the word “hexabundle” into a regular solid hexagon.

The full pointing observes sparse, independently located bundles with per-plate
orientation, not a 2° filled circle. V2 can encode one fixed compound arrangement,
but one static instrument geometry cannot substitute for variable target-assigned
plates. No planned core gate includes a full plate configuration/assignment model;
therefore D at this scope. A separately selected single-bundle nominal profile
could be useful later, without blocking release on full plate fidelity. Exact
fill, bundle boundary, coordinate/robot export mapping and plate completeness
are unresolved. Use authoritative configured fields, not an invented sky lattice.

### SAMI / SAMI Galaxy Survey — D (full plate)

The [Croom et al. 2012 instrument paper](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/421/1/872/991492)
describes 13 deployable 61-core bundles across a 1° field, 1.6″ fibre cores,
75% fill and nominal 15″ bundle diameter. A later Hector comparison quotes
15.7″ for SAMI: diameter definitions/calibration need reconciliation. The
[survey DR1 documentation](https://sami-survey.org/system/files/papers/545/sami_dr1_6.pdf)
specifies seven 1800 s object frames: the first central, then six positions
radially offset by 0.7″ at 60° intervals. These are nominal guide-camera offsets;
atmospheric refraction/dispersion changes the effective offsets across the plate,
so reconstruction uses measured alignments. Reconstructed cubes are not a
uniformly observed 1° plate.

As for Hector, the current runtime cannot derive observed sparse bundle regions
from a plate center alone. Fixed compounds support a known immutable arrangement,
not arbitrary repluggable plate configurations. Full plate semantics are D;
single-bundle planning may later use a documented envelope/dither model. Plate
and galaxy/bundle centers and orientations must remain distinct in coordinate
exports. No survey-wide overlap/lattice or target completeness is claimed. Fibre
core maps, bundle boundary and instrument-specific export remain unresolved;
they are not automatic release requirements for a narrower agreed profile.

## Wide-field imaging evidence

### S-PLUS / T80-South — A (protected baseline model)

T80Cam is the imager; S-PLUS is the survey. Its single 9.2k detector samples a
nominal 1.4° × 1.4° field at 0.55″/pixel on T80-South at CTIO:
[Mendes de Oliveira et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.01567) and
[NOIRLab Data Lab instrument table](https://datalab.noirlab.edu/data/splus).
It is not a gapped multichip mosaic. The
[STEP survey paper, §2.1](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/529/1/59/7608535)
describes small east/west offsets in the S-PLUS exposure sequence.

A here means the **published Jasytata planning contract**, not calibrated pixel
WCS, exposure depth or a dither-complete observing recipe. Keep the existing
1.4° square, unrotated compatibility geometry, `legacy_splus` placement/inference,
120″ effective overlap and `RA,DEC,EPOCH=2000` output unchanged. The overlap is
provenance from the frozen local contract in [ALGORITHM](ALGORITHM.md), not a
new universal overlap inferred from a technical paper. No new T80 profile or
survey is added. Dither sequencing, actual CCD-edge calibration and arbitrary PA
remain separate future refinements unless explicitly authorized.

### Rubin / LSSTCam / LSST — C

LSSTCam is the imager; LSST is a time-domain survey on the 8.4 m-primary Simonyi
telescope. The [Rubin instrument page](https://rubinobservatory.org/for-scientists/rubin-101/instruments)
quotes 9.6 deg². The [LSST Science Book, §2.4](https://www.lsst.org/sites/default/files/docs/sciencebook/SB_Whole.pdf)
shows the 189 science sensors and nonscience guide/wavefront sensors. The
[camera final design report](https://project.lsst.org/reviews/jdr-2019/sites/lsst.org.reviews.jdr-2019/files/LCA-11591-B_REVIEW_%28Camera_Final_Design_Report%29.pdf)
uses 9.65 deg² as a design field value. These rounded/design areas are not an
authoritative active detector union or a radius to derive an exact circle from.

A nominal envelope is C for coarse field planning, with gaps/outer shape excluded
from any exact coverage claim. Rotation and translational dithering are visit
strategy choices; [Rubin's commissioning update](https://www.rubin.community/t/2025-09-12-on-sky-commissioning-update/10953)
also shows why a historical simulation should not be treated as an immutable
observing policy. Current release-grade visit/rotation conventions, effective
area, overlap and scheduler placement must be sourced separately. Use supplied
visit centers initially. Jasytata does not reproduce LSST scheduling, depth or
visit databases; camera masks may be deferred after envelope semantics are clear.

### DECam / DES — C

DECam is a Blanco/CTIO facility camera; DES, DELVE and other programmes have
different observing policies. The [DES DR1 technical paper, §2](https://www.darkenergysurvey.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/DR1Release.pdf)
describes its approximately 3 deg² field and 62 science CCDs. The
[DES image-format documentation](https://data.darkenergysurvey.org/aux/releasenotes/DESDMrelease.html)
distinguishes an exposure mosaic, individual CCD images and image/weight planes.
Detector gaps and the stepped outer arrangement mean neither a bounding rectangle
nor a nominal circle is an exact active footprint. The area quoted is not enough
to establish an exact boundary or filling factor.

C allows coarse imaging-envelope planning with explicit gaps/edge limitations;
it must not claim that one exposure covers every source in that envelope.
Survey revisits/dithers and overlap belong to DES or the chosen programme, not
to DECam. No numeric spacing or PA policy is bundled. Use supplied centers until
the named strategy and camera orientation are established. Coordinates from
calibrated image WCS and telescope centers must not be conflated. Exact CCD masks,
status by epoch and facility observing-script export are deferred refinements.
The NOIRLab instrument page was inaccessible through the research tool; the
survey's primary technical documentation supplies the evidence here.

### Subaru / HSC / HSC-SSP — C

HSC is the camera; HSC-SSP has Wide, Deep and UltraDeep survey policies. The
[Subaru instrument page](https://subarutelescope.org/Instruments/HSC/)
gives a 1.5°-diameter optical field, 104 science CCDs plus guide/focus devices,
and notes a disabled science CCD beginning in 2025. The
[HSC-SSP survey design paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.05858)
describes the distinct survey layers. A 0.75°-radius circle therefore represents
only a nominal envelope, not the stepped/gapped, date-dependent science mosaic.
Usable union area/fill is not established from the quoted optical diameter.

This is C for broad field planning. Rotator settings, dithers, repeated pointings
and overlap must be selected from the appropriate layer/programme rather than
invented as instrument properties. Manual/imported centers are appropriate for
Gate 1; no basis is bundled. Subaru links facility planning/OPE tools separately:
Jasytata center CSV is not an OPE file, and instrument rotation should not be
inferred from lattice orientation. Exact detector geometry and run-specific masks
can be deferred when the profile explicitly promises envelope planning only.

### VST / OmegaCAM — C

OmegaCAM is the imager, not KiDS or VPHAS+. The
[ESO instrument description](https://www.eso.org/sci/facilities/paranal/instruments/omegacam/inst.html)
gives 32 science CCDs, a nominal 1° square and **91.4% geometric fill**.
The [ESO P99 user manual, §3.4.2](https://www.hq.eso.org/sci/facilities/paranal/instruments/omegacam/doc/VST-MAN-OCM-23100-3110_p99_v2.pdf)
recommends 25″/85″ dither steps for ordinary mosaic gaps, with different
requirements for segmented filters. These are detector-axis offsets, not
survey-grid overlap. Individual exposures are noncontiguous; combined tiles may
fill gaps depending on the actual sequence.

A 1° square is C, with coverage meaning a nominal field envelope and no exact
active-area claim. PA, selected filter, offset pattern and survey center list must
be specified separately; no universal overlap/lattice is established here.
V2 compounds could represent a sourced fixed detector map, but this gate does
not invent such a map from an overall fill fraction. Exact masks and filter-cross
shadows may be deferred after usable nominal-field semantics are agreed. ESO OB
exports and per-CCD FITS WCS ingestion are not supplied by these profiles.

### CFHT / MegaCam / CFHTLS — C

MegaCam is a camera in MegaPrime; CFHTLS used a historical configuration. CFHT
describes the instrument as approximately a 1° square in its
[facility factsheet](https://www.cfht.hawaii.edu/en/about/CFHT_FS_v1-2.pdf).
The [raw-data description](https://www.cfht.hawaii.edu/Instruments/Imaging/Megacam/rawdata.html)
distinguishes the pre-2015 36-readout footprint from later 40-readout operation.
The [historical PH2 guide](https://www.cfht.hawaii.edu/Instruments/Queue/ph2_tutorial_v2.html)
documents small gaps of roughly 12–14″ and connector gaps about 85″, different
dither patterns and significant field distortion. Its numeric recommendations
are historical, not automatically the current observing policy.

A nominal square is C, not exact active coverage. Fix configuration era before
selecting a footprint or claiming usable area/fill; detector ears and vignetting
cannot be silently ignored. CFHTLS overlap/dithers must come from its survey
documentation, not the camera name. Manual field centers initially avoid an
invented lattice. PA, current masks and QSO export mapping require confirmation.
The linked specifications page was inaccessible, so no more precise field
boundary is asserted. Exact chip masks may remain a refinement of a clearly
labeled envelope profile.

## Multi-object spectroscopy evidence

### VISTA / 4MOST — B (target-access envelope only)

4MOST is a facility serving multiple surveys, not one universal survey policy.
The [consortium capability table](https://www.4most.eu/cms/facility/capabilities/)
gives a hexagonal 4.2 deg² field, nominal 2.5° diameter, 2436 positioners,
1.45″ fibre apertures and 15″ minimum fibre separation. The
[ESO overview](https://www.eso.org/sci/facilities/paranal/instruments/4most/overview.html)
describes its VISTA installation and separate high/low-resolution spectrographs.
The rounded diameter's flat-to-flat/circumscribed convention and exact usable
patrol boundary are not resolved here; do not derive regular vertices from area
and diameter while assuming they are mutually exact.

One pointing means potential target access and **allocated fibre apertures**, not
contiguous spectral coverage. B requires a footprint-role/metric contract for a
usable patrol-envelope planning profile. Detailed individual patrol/collision
regions and allocation can be deferred at that scope. Full target completeness
remains unsupported. [Tempel et al.'s 4MOST tiling study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03307)
illustrates target/exposure-driven survey placement, not an intrinsic fixed basis.
Use supplied fields initially. Survey-specific overlap/revisits, PA and operational
coordinate/export conventions remain to be frozen; no science-area profile is
bundled merely because v2 accepts a hexagonal polygon.

### Subaru / PFS — B (target-access envelope only)

PFS is the instrument; SSP and open-use programmes supply target/visit strategy.
Its [official parameter table](https://pfs.ipmu.jp/research/parameters.html)
explicitly specifies a hexagonal field with ∼1.38° **circumscribed-circle**
diameter and ∼1.25 deg² area. Fibres subtend 1.12″ centrally / 1.02″ at the
edge; positioner patrol diameters are 107.4″ / 97.9″, respectively, and minimum
fibre separation is ∼30″. The page lists 2386 (2394) science fibres: retain the
configuration/status distinction rather than assume every historical positioner
is available. Subaru's [instrument list](https://subarutelescope.org/Instruments/)
separately describes PFS capability.

The field hexagon is target access, not observed spectral area. Like 4MOST, B
requires an honest role/metric contract, not a new lattice engine. A patrol
envelope may be useful without detailed Cobra patrol/collision modeling; fibre
allocation and target completeness are deferred. PA/rotator settings, exact
distorted boundary, survey tiling/revisits and facility export requirements remain
unresolved here. Manual supplied centers are appropriate until a named survey
strategy is validated. Never treat repeat field envelopes as completed spectra
for every target in the region.

carry the source, scope, PA convention and link to this evidence. The published

In the browser, assign a compatible source catalogue via its instrument selector;
rendering and mixed-instrument coverage use the existing dataset path. Arbitrary

sampling resolves KCWI. Resolution across instrument scales is a Gate 2 contract
question, with scientific validation in Gate 8; no sampling algorithm is changed
here.

## Empirical contract gaps and Gate 2 decisions

| Evidence-backed gap | Existing capability and boundary | Gate 2 decision required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Instrument-only lifecycle (KCWI and other facility instruments) | Registry already supports it; browser JSON, output selection and create-survey workflow require a fresh pair. Importing a second survey for an already registered instrument encounters duplicate instrument rejection. | Decide standalone instrument import/export, referencing existing instruments and usable manual output policy without fake named surveys. Preserve registry identity/conflict guarantees. |
| Provenance, fidelity and scope (all C cases) | `description` can carry prose and links; strict v2 has no structured source/version/fidelity/coverage-role metadata. | Decide what must travel with an exported profile and be visible with coverage metrics; free text may suffice for some metadata. Do not choose schema v3 without migration evidence. |
| Footprint role (4MOST/PFS; fibre bundles) | Binary footprint union means observed area. A valid polygon says nothing about target accessibility versus allocated apertures or reconstructed coverage. | Freeze separate roles and metric language. Select patrol-envelope scope or defer MOS; never count access as spectral completeness. Detailed patrol maps are optional at envelope scope. |
| Instrument modes/configuration (KCWI, MaNGA, MegaCam) | Separate stable instrument IDs already handle fixed modes/epochs; arbitrary plate-dependent geometry is not described. | Prefer existing IDs where sufficient. Decide whether a family/mode relation is necessary; keep full reconfigurable Hector/SAMI plates deferred unless explicitly selected with a new contract. |
| Hexagon dimensions/conventions (PPAK/MaNGA/LVM/4MOST/PFS) | Six vertices already work; no ambiguity-free native diameter field exists. Rounded dimensions may use different conventions. | Decide sourced vertices versus native regular hexagon authoring, flat/vertex conventions and orientation. Exact fibre sampling is a separate fidelity question. |
| PA (KCWI axis mappings, MUSE rotations, LVM derotation) | Profile PA rotates geometry; export may carry PA. Source `footprintForTile` resolves profile geometry rather than interpreting arbitrary row PA. | Freeze reference axes/handedness and per-pointing override rules with precedence, missing-value behavior and facility conversion evidence. Profile PA and lattice orientation remain independent. |
| Visit groups / effective coverage (CALIFA/MaNGA/LVM; imaging dithers) | Supplied centers can be individual pointings; profiles cannot encode sequence/group, offsets, rotation, repeat counts or single-versus-combined semantics. | Decide minimum declarative observing-strategy representation and coverage/export interpretation; do not introduce a scheduler or assume reconstruction equals raw aperture union. |
| Placement provenance (LVM global tiles; named imaging/MOS surveys) | Generic local basis vectors already describe independent placement, including triangular sites. They do not encode a spherical all-sky tessellation, target allocator or published tile list. | Prefer imported authoritative centers/manual mode. Select exact empirical lattices only where supported, with overlap/phase source and local-projection limits documented. No `HEX_GRID_V1`. |
| Mixed-scale sampling (KCWI versus T80/imaging) | Coverage samples follow the active output survey, not the smallest assigned source footprint. Instrument registration alone cannot guarantee resolved small-field coverage. | Decide minimum sampling-resolution evidence and user-visible limits for mixed-scale planning; any algorithm change requires its own authorized gate and regression validation. |

Gate 2 must freeze release selection and intended semantics before accepting any
delta. A/B/C/D is scope-sensitive: a Hector single-bundle profile or a MOS patrol
envelope is a different claim from full-plate observed-area support. A class-C
profile need not wait for exact detector masks; a class-D full-plate model need
not block the entire release. Reclassification requires new evidence or an
explicit scope decision, not a convenient bounding box. No gaps in this list are
implemented during Gate 1.
